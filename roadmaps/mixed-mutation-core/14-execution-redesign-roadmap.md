## Selected Foundation Replacement

The target is the native business operation plus small, explicit Flarex overhead
and the actual cost of snapshot/history, constraints, atomic publication and
recovery. Native Medusa services and Payload Local API behavior remain owners of
business semantics; their Flarex storage bindings must also preserve efficient
bounded data demands. Reusing native business code alone does not establish
native database performance.

The selected [execution checks and framework lifecycle direction](./22-execution-checks-and-framework-lifecycles-preflight.md)
adopts Convex's logical snapshot, demanded-index dependency and ordered commit
principles while preserving Flarex's Postgres authority and native framework
profiles. Admission, readiness and publication keep separate purposes and
freshness boundaries. Reducing their overhead does not require moving all hooks
after commit, making resource locks into OCC, broadening automatic retries or
removing durable seal. Those semantic changes need their own explicit decisions.

### Architecture First, Small Optimizations Afterward

Complete the selected connected architecture replacement before starting a
separate sequence of small performance optimizations. Admission, attempt-owned
metadata and snapshot, demanded readiness, contribution, settlement and recovery
must have explicit owners and lifetimes; migrate affected consumers and remove
displaced choreography within their coherent delivery slices. Existing package
scope and terminal durability decisions still govern that work.

Every implementation change must identify the selected responsibility or
lifetime it establishes, the affected consumers, and the old path it replaces.
Fewer SQL submissions, helper extraction, query fusion, batching, compiler reuse
or a faster isolated benchmark do not by themselves complete the redesign.
Defer standalone tuning that leaves the existing execution topology intact.
An acquisition mechanism may be part of a selected architectural cutover, but
its justification must be that contract rather than an isolated query saving.

Correctness fixes, connected validation, required measurements, review and cleanup
continue during the redesign. If a measurement exposes a missing architectural
boundary, correct its owner through the focused preflight. Do not turn a failed
latency gate into an indefinite loop of local optimizations while connected
architecture work remains unfinished. Identify the remaining architecture and
required decisions explicitly, and follow their delivery gates.

Report architectural cutover and performance acceptance separately. Architectural
completion requires connected consumer and failure/recovery proofs, both reviews
and retirement of displaced paths; it does not claim that latency targets pass.
After that cutover, select and measure small optimizations against the resulting
architecture. Keep all fixed performance gates and failed measurements visible;
this ordering does not waive acceptance or authorize an unapproved durability
change.

### Evidence And Design Problems

- Actual finite storage access still enters the complete running-attempt kernel
  and source verification. Purely local method calls and borrowed construction
  already avoid SQL admission; do not diagnose them as repeated root starts.
- Intermediate readiness selects source tables and connected source definitions,
  so proof work can grow with unrelated indexes and relationships. Pending seed
  hydration likewise selects more than the specific read may need.
- The retained full-proof profile checks installation catalogs and protection
  structures at runtime. The managed standalone replacement must prove its
  coverage of mixed callers before supplying a cheaper guard here.
- The evaluator already shares global pending state. Child completion, journal
  projection, freeze and verification still create multiple representations and
  canonical encodings. Source proves repetition, not its CPU share or quadratic
  growth.
- Healthy finite completion retains durable seal, finishing and publication.
  These three transactions preserve finish-only recovery; changing them is a
  durability decision, not SQL concatenation.

## Owners and invariants

- Persistence owns canonical metadata, live acceptance, database transactions,
  history, conflict validation, materialization and durable recovery. Trusted
  executor owns root orchestration, retries, cancellation and budgets. Protocol
  owns exact serialized contracts. Hosts adapt lifecycle and transport.
- Product service, selected Payload Local API access/validation/hooks and finite
  Medusa workflow steps retain their native business and lifecycle ownership.
  Do not copy them into a planner, generic CRUD engine or adapter-owned admission.
- A prepared immutable object is not a live admission verdict. Each actual DB
  boundary keeps fresh scope, placement, binding, generation, revocation,
  session/lease/claim/fence and post-lock database-time checks. A local tentative
  operation may discover persisted revocation at its next DB boundary.
- Keep one logical snapshot plus ordered pending changes, exact positive and
  negative point/predicate/page/relation/unique evidence, revision/tombstone
  history and final OCC validation. Method grants do not prove dependency closure.
- No database transaction spans sandbox execution, foreign capture, framework
  callbacks asking for the next read, resource waits, workflow suspension or
  remote effects. Plans contain already bounded trusted work, not guessed future
  callbacks. Preserve validation, failure and resource-charge order.
- Root conflict retry discards the whole tentative graph and uses a fresh
  snapshot/framework state under finite budgets. Preserve rollback-only,
  cancellation, fencing, replay and exact retained outcome resolution before
  uncertain COMMIT can cause any callback replay. Only Postgres settles.
- Slice 15 extends existing ten-table legacy Application protection with thirteen
  immutable current target tables under full existing attestation/quiescence.
  Its actual optimized graphs use the legacy Application issuer and shared
  active-head/schema/binding readers; current relation Fold callers keep full
  proof. Live/physical and separately owned control evidence remain checked.
  Separate databases have no atomic joint generation.
